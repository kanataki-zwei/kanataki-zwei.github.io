# Content Strategy & Messaging

Principles and rationale behind the copy on the site. Use this as a reference when writing or reviewing new content.

---

## Core Positioning

**Who Patrick is:**
Senior Data Engineering Manager with 12 years in tech, 10 of those in data engineering. Technically active — still close to architecture, code, and critical decisions. Manages and builds engineering teams. Works at the intersection of technical depth and strategic leadership.

**The tension to manage:**
"Senior Data Engineering Manager" signals management. The goal is to show that the management is built on deep engineering — not a career pivot away from it. The site should communicate: *this person leads engineers because they've been one for a decade, and they're still technically credible today.*

**What Patrick is NOT trying to signal:**
- A micromanager who codes everything
- A manager who's left engineering behind
- Someone actively looking for a new job

---

## Voice & Tone

- **Confident, not boastful.** Let the numbers and outcomes speak. Avoid superlatives.
- **Direct, not formal.** Write like a senior professional talking to a peer, not a CV submitted to HR.
- **Warm, not corporate.** The social impact work (Educate!, Faraja) is part of the identity — the voice should reflect someone who cares about the work, not just the credentials.
- **British English throughout.** Organisation, standardising, standardisation, colour, etc.

## Key Messages by Section

### Hero

The hero has three jobs in this order:
1. **Headline** — what you do (team + platform builder)
2. **Subtitle** — your track record and how you got here
3. **Body paragraph** — how you operate (strategic + technically close)

The stats row handles credentials. The paragraph handles character. These are intentionally separate.

**Headline principle:** Name both dimensions — building teams AND building platforms. Neither alone is sufficient.
> *"Building data engineering teams and the platforms they ship"*

**Subtitle principle:** Lead with the 10-year foundation, frame management as a natural next chapter — not a departure.
> *"10 years in data engineering, now leading the teams that build it"*

**Body principle:** The hands-on signal should be strategic and selective, not constant. The phrase "when the problem demands it" is load-bearing — it signals judgement, not compulsion.
> *"I stay technically close to the work — shaping architecture, reviewing critical decisions, and rolling up my sleeves when the problem demands it"*

### Stats Row

Each number has a specific job:
| Stat | What it signals |
|---|---|
| 12 yrs in tech | Overall career credibility and depth |
| 10 yrs in data | Specialist depth in the specific domain |
| 3 teams led | Management track record |
| 10+ enterprise warehouses shipped | Volume and scale of delivery |

"Enterprise warehouses" not just "warehouses" — these were large, complex, production systems.

### Experience

**Bullet principle:** Lead with action, follow with outcome or metric where possible.
Good: *"Designed and implemented an enterprise data warehouse... cutting time-to-insight by more than 50%"*
Avoid: *"Responsible for data warehouse work"*

**Manager entry bullets** should focus on: team building, strategy, roadmap, governance, cross-functional alignment.
**IC entry bullets** should focus on: architecture, systems built, tools deployed, technical outcomes.

Keeping these distinct is important — it shows clear progression rather than repeating the same bullets under a different title.

### Skills

**Capability categories** (Leadership, Data Architecture, Analytics, Governance, AI Engineering, ML Engineering) answer: *"What can you do?"*

**Tools & Platforms** answers: *"What do you use to do it?"*

Never mix capabilities and tools in the same chip list. A tool without capability context is a keyword. A capability without tool context is vague. Keeping them separate makes both more readable.

### Contact

**Principle:** This is not a hire-me page. The contact section should feel like an open door for conversations, not a job application.

Target prompts:
- Curiosity about what data could do for their organisation
- Questions about data strategy or AI
- General connection with someone experienced in the space

**Avoid:** Any language that implies availability for employment, urgency, or desperation.

## What to Preserve When Updating Content

1. **The 12/10 year distinction** — always communicate both numbers, never conflate them
2. **"Rolling up my sleeves when the problem demands it"** — this phrase carries the strategic-but-technical positioning; don't dilute it
3. **Social impact framing at Educate!** — "serving young people", "youth data protection", "social impact EdTech" — this is part of the identity, not just a job description
4. **British English** — applies to all copy, including bullet points and section descriptions
5. **Metric specificity** — where numbers exist (50%, 40%, 25%, $1M MRR, 85% forecasting accuracy), keep them. Vague impact claims are weaker than specific ones.
